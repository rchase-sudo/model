Forma Designs: a 97% margin product looking for its channel.

October 22, 2026 · Ryan Chase, Founder

Summary

Forma Designs sells listing agents a $949 development package for vacant land, made by in-house software for under $1.50, and is raising capital to find out what it costs to sell at scale.

The product is a conceptual site plan, a 3D rendering and a due diligence summary for one listing, with an optional $1,000 interactive property website. It is produced by Drafted, software the founder built, so each sale keeps about 97% after production and payment fees.

The business is very early. It has $1,230 in revenue from 4 clients, all at legacy $200–$500 pricing, and no completed sale yet at $949. Agents reached by phone show 90%+ interest; the gap is closing after the call.

The bet in one line: margins are already solved; demand per dial at the new price is not.

Product, pricing and Drafted

Forma sells one flat-priced package per listing, made by its own software, and plans to double the price by April 2027.

What the agent gets. A conceptual site plan fit to current zoning and setbacks, a 3D rendering, and a due diligence summary of development costs and potential returns, delivered within 7 days. One revision round is included. It is a marketing and feasibility tool, not a stamped plan, and is not for permitting or construction.

Terms. $949 upfront, or $159 a month for up to 6 payments with any balance due at closing. The card is saved through Stripe at order and charged only on delivery. If the listing is not under contract within 6 months, the next property is done free. The property website adds $1,000 ($1,949 total).

A $3,500 premium tier is planned as a price anchor.

Drafted, the engine. The full flow is live: a property description goes in and a finished package comes out. Drafted pulls the parcel, zoning and LiDAR topography, lays out a concept fit to the buildable area and parking code, builds a 3D model and photoreal render, then produces the due diligence package and optional website. It runs on Claude, image generation, Supabase and Modal workers. The founder reports AI and compute cost under $1.50 per package. Longer term, Drafted can be sold to civil engineering firms as software.

What this leaves out is the real cost: acquiring the customer. Today that is the founder’s calling time, which has no cash cost and no measured CAC. Funded, it becomes a caller’s salary, ad spend and email tooling. A sensible ceiling is about $400 of acquisition cost per $949 sale, which still pays back in the first month. Two smaller costs also sit off this table: payment-plan defaults, and free work owed under the 6-month guarantee.

Market

There are roughly 427,000 land listings for sale in the U.S. at any time, and Forma’s base case needs about 0.1% of a developable slice of them each month.

  • Realtor.com counted 426,986 land listings for sale in Q1 2026, with inventory still well below pre-pandemic levels (Realtor.com land report).
  • Zillow counted 300,242 empty lots of 5 acres or less for sale in June 2026, 17.4% of all for-sale listings (Zillow Research).
  • NAR had 1,438,569 members in June 2026, the pool of possible buyers of the package (HousingWire).

Illustrative sizing, not a forecast. If 5% of listed land is developable and worth packaging, that is about 21,000 listings on the market at any time: roughly $20M at $949, or $40M at $1,899. The 5% share is an assumption to frame scale, and listing counts are a stock, not annual volume.

Adjacent markets: Drafted sold as software to civil engineering firms, and packages sold direct to land owners and developers. Neither is in the forecast.

The free concept is itself the ad: for under $1.50, every outreach can show the agent their own listing finished. The main leak today is after the call. Interested agents go cold before ordering, so the first fix is a follow-up system: a payment link sent during the call, a second touch within 24 hours, and proposals tracked in the CRM.

Strengths

Forma’s strengths are margin, its own software and a product agents understand in one sentence.

  • Margin: about 97% contribution per sale; fixed costs under $100 a month.
  • Own software: Drafted produces in minutes what engineering firms bill weeks for.
  • Founder fit: civil engineering and land development background, and he builds the software himself.
  • Instant clarity: 90%+ of agents reached by phone show interest.
  • Pricing power: the main objection moved from “too cheap” to “maybe” as price rose from $200 to $949.
  • Low-friction offer: no deposit, charged on delivery, 7-day turnaround, 6-month guarantee.

Holes and weaknesses

The biggest hole is proof: Forma has not yet sold at its current price and has one part-time person to sell it.

  • Tiny revenue base. $1,230 from 4 clients, all at $200–$500. Zero sales at $949.
  • One part-time founder. Runs Forma alongside a day job. No team, no backup.
  • No measured CAC. Only cold calling has run, and it costs time, not cash.
  • Leads go cold. Calls go well, but there is no system that closes after the call.
  • No outcome proof. No tracked data yet that a package sells a listing faster.
  • Thin infrastructure. Framer subdomain and a Gmail sender.
  • Offer liabilities. Payment plans can default; the guarantee owes free work.
  • Copyable. AI tools lower the bar for firms and new rivals to offer something similar.
  • No E&O insurance yet against a claim that a concept or cost estimate misled a buyer.

Most of these are what outside capital would be spent fixing.

Assumptions.

  • Volume: bootstrap 300 dials a month rising slowly; funded 1,200 founder dials plus a caller ramping from 800 to 1,500; ads $1,500–$2,500 a month at $150 per lead and 5% close; a small lift from cold email.
  • Price: $949 and $1,000 website through April 2027, then $1,899 and $2,000. The doubling is assumed to cut close rates by about 40%.
  • Mix: 15% of orders add the website; 40% pay monthly, with 10% of remaining plan payments defaulting.
  • Costs: under $1.50 production, $5 per website, Stripe 2.9% + $0.30, about $100 a month fixed. Funded operating costs $9,600–$10,600 a month, including a $4,000 founder draw, a $3,000 caller base, tools and travel.
  • The stress case keeps spending unchanged, which the pull-back point is designed to prevent. Reference point: about 3% of all dials closed at $200–$500.

The plan for capital

Delivery is automated; selling is the bottleneck. Outside capital goes to selling capacity, in a set order, with each stage measured before the next starts.

The bottleneck. Drafted turns a property description into a finished package with no manual work. Every sale still depends on one person’s calling hours, and interested agents go cold after the call.

StageWhenWhat the cash doesWhat it unlocks
1. Close the leakImmediatelyFollow-up system, payment link sent on every call, real domain and email sending setup, E&O insuranceMore of today’s 90%+ call interest turns into orders
2. Add selling hoursMonth 1–2Founder goes full-time; first dedicated caller on the proven scriptDials go from about 300 to 2,000+ a month
3. Open channelsMonth 2–6Paid ads to land agents, brokerage visits, cold email at volume, each held to a CAC ceilingSales that don’t depend on phone time; a measured cost per sale for each channel
4. Scale what worksMonth 6+More callers on the channel that converts, regional field sales, a Drafted pilot with engineering firmsGrowth past one team, and a second product line

The order is the point: fix conversion first, then add hours, then test channels, then put more behind the winner. The forecast’s funded scenarios follow this plan, and every stage is reported monthly from the CRM.
